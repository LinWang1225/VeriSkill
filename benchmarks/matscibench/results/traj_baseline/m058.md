## 题目
In Cu, if we assume that each Cu atom donates one electron, the free electron concentration is $8.5 \times 10^{22} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$. (a) What is the value of $E_{\mathrm{F}, 0}$? (b) If the concentration of conduction electrons estimated by conductivity measurements is $1.5 \times 10^{23} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$, then find the number of electrons donated per Cu atom. Give your answer as a tuple (Fermi energy at 0K, number of electrons donated per Cu atom).

Refer to Table:
Fermi Energy Levels ( $E_{F, 0}$ ) and Work Functions ( $\phi$ ) for Different Metals
\begin{tabular}{|l|l|l|}
\hline Metal & Work Function ( $\phi$ ) ( eV ) & Fermi Energy Level at $0 \mathrm{~K}\left(E_{F, 0}\right)$ \\
\hline Silver & 4.26 & 5.5 \\
\hline Aluminum & 4.28 & 11.7 \\
\hline Gold & 5.1 & 5.5 \\
\hline Copper & 4.65 & 7.0 \\
\hline Lithium & 2.3 & 4.7 \\
\hline Magnesium & 3.7 & 7.1 \\
\hline Sodium & 2.75 & 3.2 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
Give your final answer in units of (eV, ).
Note: No supplementary information needed.

## 过程
(a) The Fermi energy at 0 K is given in the table for Copper: $E_{F,0} = 7.0$ eV. This is confirmed by direct calculation using the free-electron formula
$$E_{F,0} = \frac{h^2}{8m_e}\left(\frac{3n}{\pi}\right)^{2/3}$$
with $n = 8.5\times10^{22}\,\text{cm}^{-3} = 8.5\times10^{28}\,\text{m}^{-3}$, which gives $E_{F,0} \approx 7.05$ eV, consistent with the tabulated 7.0 eV.

(b) If one electron per Cu atom corresponds to $n_1 = 8.5\times10^{22}\,\text{cm}^{-3}$, and the measured concentration is $n_{\text{meas}} = 1.5\times10^{23}\,\text{cm}^{-3}$, then the number of electrons donated per Cu atom is
$$\frac{n_{\text{meas}}}{n_1} = \frac{1.5\times10^{23}}{8.5\times10^{22}} \approx 1.76.$$

## 最终答案
(7.0, 1.76)
