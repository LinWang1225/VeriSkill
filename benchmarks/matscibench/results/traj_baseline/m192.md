## 题目
Calculate the size of the critical radius and the number of atoms in the critical nucleus when solid copper forms by homogeneous nucleation. Comment on the size of the nucleus and assumptions we made while deriving the equation for the radius of the nucleus. Give your answer as a tuple: (critical_radius_cm, number_of_atoms).

For Cu, 
$$ 
\begin{aligned} 
\Delta T & =236^{\circ} \mathrm{C} \quad T_{m}=1085+273=1358 \mathrm{~K} \\ 
\Delta H_{f} & =1628 \mathrm{~J} / \mathrm{cm}^{3} \\ 
\sigma_{s l} & =177 \times 10^{-7} \mathrm{~J} / \mathrm{cm}^{2} 
\end{aligned} 
$$ .

The lattice parameter for FCC copper is $a_0=0.3615 \mathrm{~nm}$
Give your final answer in units of (cm, ).

## 过程
For homogeneous nucleation, the critical radius is found by setting $d(\Delta G)/dr = 0$ on the free-energy change for forming a spherical nucleus,

$$\Delta G = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \Delta G_v + 4\pi r^2 \sigma_{sl}$$

Using the approximation $\Delta G_v \approx -\Delta H_f \Delta T / T_m$, the critical radius is

$$r^* = \frac{2\sigma_{sl} T_m}{\Delta H_f \Delta T}$$

Substituting $\sigma_{sl}=177\times10^{-7}\,\text{J/cm}^2$, $T_m=1358\,\text{K}$, $\Delta H_f=1628\,\text{J/cm}^3$, $\Delta T=236\,\text{K}$:

$$r^* = \frac{2(177\times10^{-7})(1358)}{(1628)(236)} = 1.251\times10^{-7}\,\text{cm} \approx 1.25\,\text{nm}$$

The number of atoms in the critical nucleus: the nucleus volume is $V^*=\tfrac{4}{3}\pi(r^*)^3$. For FCC Cu, $a_0=0.3615\,\text{nm}=0.3615\times10^{-7}\,\text{cm}$, with 4 atoms per unit cell, so

$$n = \frac{V^*}{a_0^3}\times 4 \approx 694.8 \approx 695\ \text{atoms}$$

**Comment on size and assumptions:** The critical radius (~1.25 nm) is only a few atomic diameters, and the nucleus contains only a few hundred atoms. This means the continuum assumptions used in deriving $r^*$ — treating the nucleus as a sphere with a well-defined macroscopic surface energy $\sigma_{sl}$ and a volume free-energy $\Delta G_v$ — are questionable: for such a small cluster the surface energy is not a sharp macroscopic quantity, the cluster is not truly spherical, and $\Delta G_v$ is not uniform. The derivation also assumes constant $\sigma_{sl}$, isotropic properties, and that $\Delta G_v \approx -\Delta H_f\Delta T/T_m$ (small undercooling / linear approximation), none of which is strictly valid for a cluster of only ~300 atoms.
