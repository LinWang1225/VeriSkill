## 题目
In doing x-radiography of steel, assume the film can detect a variation in radiation intensity represented by $\Delta I / I_0=0.001$. What thickness variation could be detected using this system for the inspection of a $12.5-\mathrm{mm}$-thick plate of steel using a $100-\mathrm{keV}$ beam? Give your answer as a tuple: (thickness_variation_positive, thickness_variation_negative).
Give your final answer in units of (mm, mm).

## 过程
The transmitted intensity through the plate follows the Beer–Lambert law:

$$I = I_0 e^{-\mu x}$$

For iron (steel ≈ iron) at 100 keV, the NIST total mass attenuation coefficient is $\mu/\rho = 0.3717\ \mathrm{cm^2/g}$. With $\rho = 7.87\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$:

$$\mu = 0.3717 \times 7.87 = 2.925\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$$

The plate thickness is $x = 12.5\ \mathrm{mm} = 1.25\ \mathrm{cm}$, giving $\mu x = 3.657$ and $e^{\mu x}=38.73$ (transmission $I/I_0 = 0.0258$).

The film detects an intensity change $|\Delta I|/I_0 = 0.001$. Solving **exactly** (not linearizing, since the problem asks for the positive and negative variations separately):

- **Thicker region** (intensity drops by $0.001\,I_0$):  
  $e^{-\mu(x+\Delta x_+)} = e^{-\mu x} - 0.001 \;\Rightarrow\; \Delta x_+ = -\tfrac{1}{\mu}\ln\!\left(1 - 0.001\,e^{\mu x}\right)$
- **Thinner region** (intensity rises by $0.001\,I_0$):  
  $\Delta x_- = -\tfrac{1}{\mu}\ln\!\left(1 + 0.001\,e^{\mu x}\right)$

Numerically (Python):
- $\Delta x_+ = -\tfrac{1}{2.925}\ln(1 - 0.03873) = +0.13503\ \mathrm{mm}$
- $\Delta x_- = -\tfrac{1}{2.925}\ln(1 + 0.03873) = -0.12990\ \mathrm{mm}$

The two are slightly asymmetric because the exponential response is nonlinear.

## 最终答案
(0.135, -0.130)
