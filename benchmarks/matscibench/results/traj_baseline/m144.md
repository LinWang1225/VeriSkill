## 题目
(a) Demonstrate that the CPR is related to the corrosion current density $i\left(A / \mathrm{cm}^{2}\right)$ through the expression

$$
\mathrm{CPR}=\frac{K A i}{n \rho}
$$

where $K$ is a constant, $A$ is the atomic weight of the metal experiencing corrosion, $n$ is the number of electrons associated with the ionization of each metal atom, and $\rho$ is the density of the metal.
(b) Calculate the value of the constant $K$ for the CPR in mpy and $i$ in $\mu A / \mathrm{cm}^{2}\left(10^{-6} \mathrm{~A} / \mathrm{cm}^{2}\right)$.

## 过程
**(a) Derivation**

Consider a corroding metal surface of unit area (1 cm²). The corrosion current density $i$ (A/cm²) represents charge passing per unit area per unit time. By Faraday's law, the molar rate at which metal atoms ionize per unit area is

$$
\frac{\text{mol}}{\text{cm}^2\cdot\text{s}}=\frac{i}{nF}
$$

where $F=96500\ \text{C/mol}$ is Faraday's constant. Multiplying by the atomic weight $A$ gives the mass loss rate per unit area:

$$
\dot m = \frac{Ai}{nF}\quad \left[\frac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^2\cdot\text{s}}\right]
$$

Dividing by the density $\rho$ (g/cm³) converts mass loss to a volume loss per unit area per unit time, which is exactly the penetration velocity (cm/s):

$$
\text{CPR}\ [\text{cm/s}]=\frac{Ai}{nF\rho}
$$

Rearranging into the form $\mathrm{CPR}=\dfrac{K\,A\,i}{n\rho}$ gives $K=1/F$ when CPR is in cm/s and $i$ in A/cm², demonstrating the stated relationship.

**(b) Evaluate $K$ for CPR in mpy and $i$ in $\mu$A/cm²**

Let $i'$ be the current in $\mu$A/cm², so $i=i'\times10^{-6}$ A/cm². Convert cm/s → mpy (mils/year):
- $1\ \text{cm}=\dfrac{1000}{2.54}$ mils
- $1\ \text{yr}=24\times365\times3600=31\,536\,000$ s

Thus

$$
K = 10^{-6}\cdot\frac{1000}{2.54}\cdot\frac{31\,536\,000}{96500}
   = \frac{31\,536}{2.54\times 96500}
$$

Numerically: $K = 0.1287 \approx 0.129$.

## 最终答案
0.129
