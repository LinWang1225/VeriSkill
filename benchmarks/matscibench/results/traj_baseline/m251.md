## 题目
The electrical resistivity of a beryllium alloy containing $5 \mathrm{at} \%$ of an alloying element is found to be $50 \times 10^{-6} \mathrm{ohm} \cdot \mathrm{cm}$ at $400^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$. Determine the contributions to resistivity due to temperature and due to impurities by finding the expected resistivity of pure beryllium at $400^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$, the resistivity due to impurities, and the defect resistivity coefficient. What would be the electrical resistivity if the beryllium contained $10 \mathrm{at} \%$ of the alloying element at $200^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$? Give your answer as a tuple: (resistivity_pure_Be_400C, resistivity_impurities, defect_resistivity_coefficient, resistivity_10at_percent_200C).
Give your final answer in units of (ohm·cm, ohm·cm, ohm·cm, ohm·cm).

## 过程
The computation yields clean values, confirming the standard textbook properties for beryllium: ρ(20°C) = 4.0×10⁻⁶ ohm·cm and temperature coefficient α = 0.025 /°C.

## 过程

**Matthiessen's rule:** ρ_total = ρ(T) + ρ_impurity, where ρ_impurity = β·c (β = defect resistivity coefficient, c = atom fraction).

**Pure Be temperature dependence (linear):** ρ(T) = ρ₂₀[1 + α(T − 20°C)], with ρ₂₀ = 4.0×10⁻⁶ ohm·cm and α = 0.025 /°C for beryllium.

**Step 1 — Pure Be at 400°C:**
ρ_pure(400) = 4.0×10⁻⁶ [1 + 0.025×(400−20)] = 4.0×10⁻⁶ [1 + 9.5] = 4.0×10⁻⁶ × 10.5 = **42×10⁻⁶ ohm·cm**

**Step 2 — Impurity contribution (5 at% alloy, ρ = 50×10⁻⁶ at 400°C):**
ρ_impurity = ρ_alloy − ρ_pure(400) = 50×10⁻⁶ − 42×10⁻⁶ = **8×10⁻⁶ ohm·cm**

**Step 3 — Defect resistivity coefficient:**
β = ρ_impurity / c = 8×10⁻⁶ / 0.05 = **160×10⁻⁶ ohm·cm**

**Step 4 — 10 at% alloy at 200°C:**
- Pure Be at 200°C: ρ_pure(200) = 4.0×10⁻⁶ [1 + 0.025×(200−20)] = 4.0×10⁻⁶ × 5.5 = 22×10⁻⁶ ohm·cm
- Impurity (10 at%): β·c = 160×10⁻⁶ × 0.10 = 16×10⁻⁶ ohm·cm
- Total: ρ = 22×10⁻⁶ + 16×10⁻⁶ = **38×10⁻⁶ ohm·cm**

## 最终答案
(4.2e-05, 8e-06, 0.00016, 3.8e-05)
