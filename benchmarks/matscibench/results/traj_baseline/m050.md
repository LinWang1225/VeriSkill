## 题目
The densities for three iron oxides are FeO $\left(5.70 \mathrm{Mg} / \mathrm{m}^3\right), \mathrm{Fe}_3 \mathrm{O}_4\left(5.18 \mathrm{Mg} / \mathrm{m}^3\right)$, and $\mathrm{Fe}_2 \mathrm{O}_3$ $\left(5.24 \mathrm{Mg} / \mathrm{m}^3\right)$. Calculate the Pilling-Bedworth ratio for iron relative to each type of oxide, and comment on the implications for the formation of a protective coating. Give your answer as a tuple: (pilling_bedworth_ratio_FeO, pilling_bedworth_ratio_Fe3O4, pilling_bedworth_ratio_Fe2O3).

## 过程
The Pilling–Bedworth ratio (PBR) is

$$\mathrm{PBR}=\frac{M_{\text{oxide}}\,\rho_{\text{metal}}}{n\,M_{\text{metal}}\,\rho_{\text{oxide}}}$$

where $n$ is the number of metal atoms in the oxide formula, $M_{\text{oxide}}$ and $M_{\text{metal}}$ are molar masses, and $\rho$ are densities. For iron, $M_{\text{Fe}}=55.845\ \text{g/mol}$ and $\rho_{\text{Fe}}=7.87\ \text{Mg/m}^3$.

- **FeO**: $M=71.845$, $n=1$, $\rho=5.70$ → $\mathrm{PBR}=\frac{71.845\times7.87}{1\times55.845\times5.70}=1.776$
- **Fe₃O₄**: $M=231.535$, $n=3$, $\rho=5.18$ → $\mathrm{PBR}=\frac{231.535\times7.87}{3\times55.845\times5.18}=2.100$
- **Fe₂O₃**: $M=159.690$, $n=2$, $\rho=5.24$ → $\mathrm{PBR}=\frac{159.690\times7.87}{2\times55.845\times5.24}=2.147$

All three ratios exceed 1, meaning the oxide occupies a larger volume than the metal consumed, so the oxide can fully cover the surface. A protective (passive) coating generally requires $1<\mathrm{PBR}<2$–3: FeO (1.78) is comfortably in the protective range, while Fe₃O₄ (2.10) and Fe₂O₃ (2.15) are slightly above the ideal upper bound, indicating compressive stress and a tendency toward cracking/spallation, though they still form continuous films.

## 最终答案
(1.776, 2.100, 2.147)
