## 题目
Compare the percent increase in the yield strength of commercially pure annealed aluminum, magnesium, and copper by strain hardening. Explain the differences observed. Give your answer as a tuple: (percent_increase_Al, percent_increase_Mg, percent_increase_Cu).The units of each required answer are (%, %, %), respectively.

## 过程
## Reasoning

Using the standard yield strength data from Callister's *Materials Science and Engineering* (Table 7.2) for commercially pure metals in annealed and cold-worked (strain-hardened) conditions:

| Metal | Annealed YS (MPa) | Cold-worked YS (MPa) | % Increase |
|-------|-------------------|----------------------|------------|
| Aluminum (1100) | 35 | 150 | (150−35)/35 × 100 = **328.6%** |
| Magnesium | 20 | 115 | (115−20)/20 × 100 = **475%** |
| Copper (C11000) | 69 | 365 | (365−69)/69 × 100 = **429%** |

## Explanation of Differences

**Magnesium (475% — largest increase):** Mg has an HCP crystal structure with only 3 primary slip systems at room temperature. Its annealed yield strength is extremely low (20 MPa), so even moderate strain hardening produces the largest *percent* increase — the very low starting baseline amplifies the ratio.

**Copper (429% — large increase):** Cu is FCC (12 slip systems) with **low stacking fault energy** (~40–80 mJ/m²). Low SFE means widely separated partial dislocations, making cross-slip difficult. This promotes extensive dislocation tangling and forest hardening, giving copper a high strain-hardening rate (n ≈ 0.54).

**Aluminum (328.6% — smallest increase):** Al is also FCC, but has **high stacking fault energy** (~166 mJ/m²). High SFE means narrowly separated dislocations that cross-slip easily, allowing dislocations to bypass tangles rather than pile up. This reduces the strain-hardening rate (n ≈ 0.20), yielding the smallest percent increase despite having the same crystal structure as copper.

The key differentiators are: (1) **crystal structure** — FCC (Al, Cu) has more slip systems than HCP (Mg); (2) **stacking fault energy** — Cu's low SFE gives it greater strain-hardening capacity than Al; and (3) **initial annealed strength** — Mg's very low baseline makes its percent increase the largest despite limited slip systems.

$$\boxed{(328.6,\ 475,\ 429)}$$

## 最终答案
(328.6,\ 475,\ 429)
