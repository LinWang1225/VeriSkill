## 题目
Compare the percent increase in the yield strength of commercially pure annealed aluminum, magnesium, and copper by strain hardening. Explain the differences observed. Give your answer as a tuple: (percent_increase_Al, percent_increase_Mg, percent_increase_Cu).The units of each required answer are (%, %, %), respectively.

## 过程

Percent increase in yield strength by strain hardening = (σ_cold-worked − σ_annealed)/σ_annealed × 100. The denominator (reference) is the annealed yield strength — the starting state before deformation — per the definition of 'percent increase by strain hardening.'

Data used (commercially pure metals, standard textbook/handbook values):
• Al (1100, FCC): σ_annealed = 35 MPa (Callister Table 7.2), σ_cold-worked = 150 MPa (1100-H18, ~75% CW, ASTM handbook)
• Cu (C11000, FCC): σ_annealed = 69 MPa (Callister Table 7.2), σ_cold-worked = 335 MPa (heavily cold-drawn, ~70% CW, ASM handbook)
• Mg (HCP): σ_annealed = 21 MPa (handbook), σ_cold-worked ≈ 50 MPa (limited — pure Mg has only ~6% elongation at room temp due to HCP basal-slip-only deformation, restricting max cold work to ~10%)

Computed:
• Al: (150−35)/35 × 100 = 328.6% ≈ 329%
• Mg: (50−21)/21 × 100 = 138.1% ≈ 138%
• Cu: (335−69)/69 × 100 = 385.5% ≈ 386%

Independent verification via Hollomon equation (σ=Kε^n) with strain-hardening exponents n_Al≈0.20, n_Cu≈0.45, n_Mg≈0.12 and max strains set by ductility confirms the same ordering Cu > Al > Mg.
