## 题目
Both sides of an alumina plate are coated with a metal electrode. The surface area of the electrode is $5 \mathrm{~mm}^{2}$ and the relative dielectric constant and dielectric strength of the alumina are 9.1 and 17 volt $/ \mathrm{mm}$, respectively. What is the maximum amount of electric charge that can be stored on the alumina plate's surface?The unit of the answer is F.

## 过程
## Reasoning

The alumina plate with electrodes on both sides forms a parallel-plate capacitor. The maximum charge is limited by the dielectric strength (breakdown field).

**Capacitance:**
$$C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r A}{d}$$

**Maximum voltage** (before dielectric breakdown):
$$V_{max} = E_{max} \cdot d$$

**Maximum charge:**
$$Q_{max} = C \cdot V_{max} = \frac{\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r A}{d} \cdot E_{max} \cdot d = \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r A \cdot E_{max}$$

Notice the plate thickness $d$ **cancels out**, so it isn't needed.

**Substituting values:**
- $\varepsilon_0 = 8.854 \times 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{F/m}$
- $\varepsilon_r = 9.1$
- $A = 5\ \mathrm{mm^2} = 5 \times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^2}$
- $E_{max} = 17\ \mathrm{V/mm} = 1.7 \times 10^{4}\ \mathrm{V/m}$

$$Q_{max} = (8.854\times10^{-12})(9.1)(5\times10^{-6})(1.7\times10^{4})$$
$$Q_{max} \approx 6.85 \times 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{C}$$

$$\boxed{6.85 \times 10^{-12}}$$

## 最终答案
6.85 \times 10^{-12}
