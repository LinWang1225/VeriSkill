## 题目
$\alpha$-titanium at $20^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ has an hcp crystal structure with $a=0.2950 \mathrm{~nm}$ and $c=0.4683 \mathrm{~nm}$ at $20^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$. At $900^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$, it transforms to $\beta$-titanium with a bcc crystal structure with $a=0.332$. The average coefficient of thermal expansion of $\alpha$-titanium is $8.4 \times 10^{-6} \mathrm{~K}^{-1}$. Calculate the $\%$ volume change as $\beta$-titanium transforms to $\alpha$-titanium on cooling.
The unit of the answer is %.

## 过程
## Step-by-Step Reasoning

**Goal:** Find the % volume change when β-Ti (at 900°C) transforms to α-Ti on cooling. Both phases must be compared **at the transformation temperature (900°C)**.

### 1. Bring α-Ti dimensions from 20°C up to 900°C

The linear thermal expansion factor over ΔT = 900 − 20 = 880 K:

$$1 + \alpha\Delta T = 1 + (8.4\times10^{-6})(880) = 1.007392$$

So at 900°C:
- $a_\alpha = 0.2950 \times 1.007392$ nm
- $c_\alpha = 0.4683 \times 1.007392$ nm

### 2. Volume per atom of α-Ti (hcp) at 900°C

The hcp conventional cell $V = \tfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}a^2 c$ contains 6 atoms, so per atom:

$$V_\alpha = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\,a_\alpha^2\, c_\alpha = 0.018041\ \text{nm}^3$$

### 3. Volume per atom of β-Ti (bcc) at 900°C

The bcc conventional cell $V = a^3$ contains 2 atoms, so per atom:

$$V_\beta = \frac{a_\beta^3}{2} = \frac{(0.332)^3}{2} = 0.018297\ \text{nm}^3$$

### 4. Percent volume change (β → α)

$$\%\Delta V = \frac{V_\alpha - V_\beta}{V_\beta}\times 100 = \frac{0.018041 - 0.018297}{0.018297}\times 100 \approx -1.40\%$$

The negative sign indicates a **contraction** (α is denser than β), which is expected since α-Ti is the close-packed hcp phase.

$$\boxed{-1.40}$$

## 最终答案
-1.40
