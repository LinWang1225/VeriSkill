## 题目
What is the minimum cation-to-anion radius ratio for a coordination number of 4?

## 激活技能
（未使用技能）

## 过程
The radius-ratio rule gives the minimum cation-to-anion radius ratio for a given coordination number as the critical value where the anions just touch each other while the cation simultaneously touches all anions.

For coordination number 4 (tetrahedral site): place four anions of radius R_A at the vertices of a regular tetrahedron and the cation of radius R_c at its center.

1. Anion-anion contact (critical condition): the tetrahedron edge equals 2R_A, so a = 2R_A.
2. The circumradius (center-to-vertex distance) of a regular tetrahedron is R_circ = a√6/4.
3. Cation-anion contact: R_c + R_A = R_circ = (2R_A)√6/4 = R_A√6/2.
4. Therefore R_c/R_A = √6/2 − 1 = (√6 − 2)/2.

Python evaluation: (√6 − 2)/2 = 0.22474487139158894 ≈ 0.225.

Neither g-matsci-dimensionality-geometry-match (slip-plane/Orowan 2D-vs-3D exponent mismatch) nor g-matsci-unitcell-formula-unit-scaling (per-unit-cell Z scaling) applies: this is a pure radius-ratio geometry derivation with no volume-fraction exponent and no per-unit-cell/per-formula-unit ambiguity.

（执行证据）
For tetrahedral (CN=4) coordination the critical geometry has four anions touching at tetrahedron vertices (edge a=2R_A) with the cation at the center touching all four, so R_c+R_A = a√6/4 = R_A√6/2. Solving gives R_c/R_A = (√6−2)/2, which Python evaluates to 0.22474487139158894 ≈ 0.225.

## 最终答案
\dfrac{\sqrt{6}-2}{2} \approx 0.225
